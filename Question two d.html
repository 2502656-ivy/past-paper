Importance of the alt Attribute in the <img> Tag

What it is

The alt attribute gives a short text description of an image. The browser shows this text if the image cannot load, and screen readers read it aloud to users who cannot see the image.

Four Consequences of 80% of Images Missing Alt Text

1. Visually impaired users

Screen readers cannot describe the image. They may skip it or read out the file name (e.g. "IMG_2043.jpg"). Blind users miss important health information such as charts, posters and vaccination instructions.

2. Users with slow or limited internet

In areas with poor connectivity or users saving data bundles, images may fail to load or be turned off. Without alt text they see blank boxes and cannot tell what the image was meant to show.

3. Search engines

Search engines cannot see images, so they depend on alt text to understand them. Without it, the website's images do not appear in search results and the pages rank lower. Fewer people find the health information.

4. Users of text-only or older browsers and devices

Users on basic phones or text-only browsers cannot display images. Alt text is the only way they can understand what the image is about.

Conclusion

Alt text makes the website accessible, usable on poor connections and easier to find. A public health website should provide it for every meaningful image so that all citizens can access important health information.
